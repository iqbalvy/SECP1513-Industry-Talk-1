## 📝 Executive Summary
This talk highlighted the importance of combining **Technology** and **Business** thinking to solve real-world problems and create continuous value for users[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span). Writing good code alone is not enough—understanding maintenance costs, risks, and user needs is essential to building products that attract target customers willing to pay for them.

### 💡 Key Takeaways
* **The Developer Paradox (Developer vs. CEO):** Developers focus on building and technical work, whereas CEOs focus on long-term operations, capital, and business growth.
* **TIS in Practice:** Effective information systems integrate **data, software, hardware, and people**.
* **User vs. Buyer:** Users look for convenience, while buyers prioritize **ROI, security, and transparency**.
* **AI & Human Accountability:** AI speeds up development processes, while human trust, experience, and accountability remain essential.
* **Q&A Highlight — Biggest Challenge Shifting from Developer to CEO:** Managing capital (such as getting clients to pay or renew subscriptions) and developing business skills beyond coding.

## 💭 Reflection
This talk reinforced that ICT is not just about writing code, but about solving real-world challenges. Looking at how the **myReds** application connects residents, visitors, security guards, and management on a single platform demonstrated how technology creates practical impact.

Key takeaways for my future studies and career include:
1. **Post-Launch Responsibility:** A developer's responsibility does not end after launching an app—maintaining **data privacy and system reliability** is critical.
2. **Human Accountability:** Even with AI tools accelerating workflows, human oversight and accountability remain irreplaceable.
3. **Business + Technical Mindset:** Balancing technical execution with user needs and operational sustainability leads to stronger, more impactful solutions.
